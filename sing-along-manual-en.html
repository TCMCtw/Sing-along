Sing-Along Pitch Trainer — Manual

Practice Room · MIDI / audio sing-along

What it does

Import a MIDI file or a single-part audio recording, choose the part you sing, and sing along with the others. The microphone is compared with the target pitch in real time and shows the deviation in cents, a pitch trace and a spectrum. You can change speed and key, jump anywhere, and loop a passage (A-B). You can also import a MusicXML score to read along.

Quick start

  1. Tap the upload area and import MIDI (one file with all parts, or one file per part).
  2. Pick the part you sing (“My part”).
  3. Press “🎵 Start note” to hear your first note.
  4. Put on headphones, press “🎤 Mic” and allow access.
  5. Press “▶ Play” and sing; watch the deviation display.

1. Importing

MIDI files (.mid / .midi)

  • One file with all parts, or one file per part; multi-select and repeated adding both work.
  • A file with a single track using several channels (format 0) is split by channel; percussion is skipped.
  • Part names come from track names (file names if missing). The original BPM is read from the file (initial tempo).
  • MIDI files with SMPTE timing are not supported.

Single-part audio (mp3 / wav / m4a…)

  • Use the second upload area. The pitch is detected automatically and cut into notes that become the targets; a thin white line shows the detected pitch curve and the part card shows “voiced xx%”.
  • Meant for one voice, no accompaniment. With other voices or instruments the detection jumps around.
  • Detection range is about 80–1000 Hz. Very wide vibrato (about ±50 cents or more) may split a note into pieces.
  • Targets are the pitch actually sung in the recording. To snap them to the nearest semitone, tick “Snap detected targets to semitones” before importing.
  • Audio has no BPM; use percentages for speed.
  • MP3s with harmony or accompaniment: tick “Accompaniment only” before importing. Even if you forget, when no single melody can be found the app imports the file as accompaniment and says so in orange under the import box. An accompaniment part supports tempo, key (the processed real voice plays), A-B loop, volume and pan, but it has no target pitch, cannot be compared with your voice and cannot be chosen as “My part”.
  • If the detection looks unreliable (low voiced ratio or very fragmented notes) the part card shows a ⚠ warning; re-import it with “Accompaniment only”.

Score (.musicxml / .mxl, optional)

  • Use the third upload box, "Import score". It accepts .musicxml, .mxl or .xml. The score is only for reading while you sing: MIDI and audio are what you hear, the score is what you see, and you need to match them to the same piece yourself.
  • After import a "🎼 Score" row appears: pick which part to show from the drop-down, and hide the score with the Show checkbox. A small cursor moves note by note with playback and the score scrolls with it.
  • The score does not affect playback, speed, key or pitch detection. You can skip it.
  • If you only have a PDF: the app cannot read PDF directly. Convert it to MusicXML first with other software (for example music-recognition software, or MuseScore import), and check the notes and lyrics yourself before importing. Recognition from PDF can contain mistakes.

2. Part settings

  • My part: the selected part is the target for pitch comparison.
  • Hear my part (guide tone): on by default so you hear your own part as a guide; untick it to mute your part and test yourself.
  • Each part has mute, remove, volume (0–150%) and pan. They apply live while playing, as does switching “My part”.
  • Audio parts also have an “Original” switch: on = the recording itself (with lyrics); off = tones synthesized from the detected pitch.

3. Speed and key

  • Speed %: 25–200%, 100% = original. For MIDI the original BPM is shown and you can type a “Target BPM”; the percentage follows. The current BPM and the song length are shown.
  • Transposition: −12 to +12 semitones, −50 to +50 cents; hold − / + to repeat.
  • Speed and key are applied when you press Play (or jump); if you change them during playback, drag the slider or press Play again.

4. Audio-part quality

  • With a speed or key change, the real voice (with lyrics) of audio parts is processed first, so Play may wait a moment while progress is shown. Results are cached until you change the settings.
  • Engines: “High quality” (default; more accurate pitch, slower, roughly 20–60 s per 4 minutes, estimate) and “Standard” (faster). “Keep voice timbre” compensates vowel colour when transposing.
  • High quality needs AudioWorklet; otherwise the app switches to Standard. If both fail, synthesized tones are played instead and you are told.

5. Start position and start note

  • Start slider: drag to jump anywhere; while playing it restarts from that spot. Stop then Play resumes where you stopped. “⏮ Start” rewinds; reaching the end also rewinds.
  • 🎵 Start note: plays a ~2.7 s pure tone of the first note of your part after the current start position, with transposition applied. The note name, frequency and offset are shown (e.g. C4 +20 ¢). With a loop active and the start outside it, the first note after A is used.

Count-in

  • Right under the Play / Mic row there is a "Count-in" line. When ticked, pressing "▶ Play" plays 4 clicks first (the first is higher) and then starts. The tick state is remembered.
  • The BPM number next to it is the click tempo. It follows the Target BPM or the Speed % automatically, and you can also type your own value.
  • Where the starting tempo comes from: the MIDI file's BPM; if there is no MIDI but a score is imported, the score's tempo; with audio only and no score, 90 by default.
  • The count-in plays only when you press Play. Dragging the position slider, setting or nudging A / B (+ / −), toggling the loop, clearing, and the A-B loop wrapping back to A do not play any click.
  • Untick the box to turn it off.

6. A-B loop

  1. While playing or stopped, press “Set A” where the passage begins and “Set B” where it ends (rounded to 0.1 s).
  2. Press “🔁 Loop”: it plays A→B and goes back to A repeatedly; the repeats are scheduled ahead so there is no gap.
  3. Fine-tune A and B with the − / + buttons (0.1 s, hold to repeat). “Clear” removes A and B and turns the loop off.
  • The orange tint on the display shows the loop range; your pitch trace is cleared at every return to A.
  • Changing A, B or the loop switch during playback restarts from the current position (a pause of about 0.3 s).

7. Microphone and display

  • Wear headphones, otherwise the mic picks up the accompaniment and the pitch detection is confused.
  • The mic needs an https address (GitHub Pages is fine) and permission; opening a local file or some in-app preview windows is refused. Echo cancellation and noise suppression are switched off so they do not disturb the pitch.
  • Big number = your deviation from the target: green ≤10 ¢, yellow ≤25 ¢, red >25 ¢, grey = no target now. It is folded to the nearest octave, so a man singing an octave lower is not penalised.
  • Top view: orange lines = target notes, a continuous line = your pitch (same colours; it breaks where no voice is detected), a short horizontal line per note = its mean pitch with a pale band = its spread (coloured by the mean deviation), white line = now, faint grid = semitones with note names; for audio parts a thin white curve is the detected pitch; the window shows about 2 s back to 6 s ahead.
  • Line of text under the big number: “This note” = mean deviation and spread so far for the note you are singing; “Previous” = the result for the note you just finished. Mean = average of the deviations, spread = standard deviation (±); each note is counted from 0.12 s after it starts to skip the attack. For example “Mean +12 ¢, Spread ±18 ¢” means you are 12 ¢ sharp overall and wobble within about that range. It restarts at every return to A or new Play.
  • Spectrum: orange curve = your voice, white dashed line = target frequency.

8. Notes and limits

  • MIDI targets are 12-tone equal temperament; just-intonation targets are not supported yet.
  • Pitch detection is for a single voice (about 80–1000 Hz); several voices or other sounds make it unreliable.
  • On phones keep the screen on; locking the screen may pause audio.
  • Desktop Chrome/Edge remember the folder you used last.

Troubleshooting

SymptomWhat to do
I cannot hear my partTick “Hear my part (guide tone)”; check the part is not muted and its volume is not 0.
The mic does nothingCheck the address is https, microphone access is allowed, and wear headphones.
The deviation jumps aroundToo much noise, accompaniment leaking into the mic, or too far from the mic; use headphones and move closer.
Audio detection is fragmentedThe recording has accompaniment or noise; use a clean single-voice recording.
Long wait after changing speedAudio parts must be processed; choose the Standard engine or use shorter files.
Files greyed out in the pickerThey may be in the cloud; download them first, or tap the area and choose again.
After importing an mp3 there is no part and Play etc. stay greyed outThe file was skipped. Read the orange message under the import box; an mp3 with harmony is now imported as accompaniment. If nothing was added, check that the file really is audio.
After a speed/key change an audio part sounds electronic, with no lyricsNormally the processed real voice (with lyrics) plays. Read the two lines under Play: they say “processed voice” or give the failure reason. If they say “synth”, check the part’s “Original” switch is on.
